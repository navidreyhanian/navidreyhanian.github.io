Joint Spatial and Spectral Hybrid Precoding for Multi-User MIMO-OFDM Systems

Hybrid precoding that meets OFDM spectral-emission masks and clipping limits by design and tolerates phase-shifter errors. mmWave MU-MIMO-OFDM, 28 GHz.

Paper: N. Reyhanian, R. Ghaderi Zefreh, P. Ramezani, and E. Björnson, “Joint Spatial and Spectral Hybrid Precoding for Multi-User MIMO-OFDM Systems,” arXiv:2509.07229 (PDF). Part of this work was presented at IEEE ICC 2026, Glasgow, UK.

Why it matters for modem and RF system design

  • Emission compliance is part of the design, not a check afterward. Wireless standards limit out-of-band emissions through the ACLR and the spectral emission mask. Here the mask is a constraint inside the precoder optimization, applied block-averaged in the same way 3GPP NR defines ACLR and PSD compliance.
  • Peak control for DACs and PAs. OFDM peaks put strict requirements on DACs and power amplifiers and cause distortion and spectral regrowth. A per-antenna clipping limit is enforced on the oversampled, CP-inclusive waveform.
  • Hardware-efficient and impairment-aware. A partially-connected hybrid array uses one RF chain per subarray. The phase-shifter updates are robust to random phase errors.
  • Low complexity. Every sub-step is closed-form or a bisection search inside a scalable ADMM. Convergence and per-iteration complexity are analyzed.

System architecture

End-to-end partially-connected MU-MIMO-OFDM chain. The AP applies a per-subcarrier digital precoder, then IFFT + CP, a DAC, and an RF chain feeding one phase shifter per antenna in its subarray. Each user applies phase-shifter RF combining, an ADC, CP removal + FFT, and a per-subcarrier digital combiner. The design variables are the digital and RF precoders and combiners, under power, clipping, and emission-mask limits and with phase-shifter errors.

Approach

The problem is to maximize the downlink sum-rate over all subcarriers. It is nonconvex in every variable. We developed a WMMSE-based block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm that updates the blocks in Gauss-Seidel order:

  • Digital combiners and MSE weights: closed-form updates.
  • Digital precoder: with block-level precoding (precoders fixed over a scheduling block), the subproblem has a convex objective and three convex constraints: power, spectral mask, and clipping. A three-block ADMM splits it into smaller problems, each solved in closed form or by bisection.
  • RF precoder and RF combiners: coordinate descent with a closed-form update per phase shifter. A robust variant maximizes the expected sum-rate under random phase errors \(\Delta\theta \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \sigma_e^2)\).
  • Guarantees: the inner solvers are theoretically supported, and the BCD gives a monotonically convergent regularized WMMSE objective under exact inner solves.

Results

Spectrum shaped to the mask. The expected PSD of the transmitted 20 MHz OFDM symbol stays under every enforced emission mask (red, dashed) from −60 to −20 dBm/100 kHz. The precoder trades in-band power and occupied width for compliance automatically.
A tunable rate–emissions trade-off. Going from a −60 to a −50 dBm/100 kHz mask raises OOB emissions from −48.9 to −39.1 dBm and the sum-rate from 8.69 to 15.54 bit/s/Hz (Ps = 25 dBm, NRF = 16).
When the mask, not power, is the bottleneck. Under a −20 dBm/100 kHz mask the rate keeps scaling with power and RF chains, reaching about 60 bit/s/Hz with 32 RF chains at 25 dBm. Under −40 dBm/100 kHz it saturates beyond about 15 dBm as the mask constraints become active.
Beats published RF designs. At 25 dBm, the closed-form phase-shifter updates deliver +10.5% (8 RF chains) and +14.5% (16 RF chains) more sum-rate than Wang et al.'s coordinate descent. They gain up to +6.2% over Riemannian conjugate gradient (RCG; 16 RF chains, 20 dBm) and +30–66% over random phases across all powers.
Built for imperfect hardware. With ideal phase shifters, the proposed design beats classical hybrid MMSE by 9.5–20%. With 30° phase-shifter errors, the robust update rules add 23–38% over the non-robust WMMSE design and 40–48% over non-robust hybrid MMSE (NRF = 8).
Fast, monotone convergence. The sum-rate rises with every BCD iteration, and most of the gain arrives in the first ~10 iterations (25 dBm per subcarrier, 64 subcarriers).
The objective tracks the rate. The WMMSE cost keeps falling with power under the −20 dBm/100 kHz mask and levels off once the −40 dBm/100 kHz mask binds, mirroring the sum-rate.

Simulation testbed

Implemented in Python with an object-oriented design, using:

  • Numerical computing: NumPy and SciPy on optimized BLAS/LAPACK
  • Channel modeling: NVIDIA Sionna on TensorFlow
  • Parallelization: multiprocessing (process pools across all CPU cores) and concurrent.futures
  • Plotting: Matplotlib

Size: about 23,300 lines of Python across 44 modules, of which about 14,300 are code.

Simulation parameters

  • Arrays and users: 32-antenna BS; 4 users with 4 antennas and 2 RF chains each, sharing all subcarriers. Half-wavelength ULAs.
  • Band: 28 GHz carrier, 20 MHz bandwidth, 64 subcarriers.
  • Geometry and channel: users within a 4 m radius, 400 m from the BS. Frequency-selective Rician channel (\(\kappa\) = 10 dB) with 3GPP path loss and shadowing.
  • Noise: −174 dBm/Hz with an 8 dB noise figure.
  • Constraints: clipping limit \(\chi = 0.7\). Clipping and mask constraints are met with at least 90% probability, and the mask is enforced from 10.01 to 20 MHz on each side of the band.
