Precoding for Uplink RIS-Assisted Cell-Free MIMO-OFDM Systems with IQ Imbalance

Joint UE precoding, multi-RIS configuration, and BS combining that account for transmit and receive IQ imbalance and the image-subcarrier leakage it causes in OFDM. 28 GHz.

Why it matters for modem and RF system design

  • IQ imbalance is a real transceiver impairment. Low-cost wideband radios at the UEs and BSs mismatch the in-phase and quadrature branches. In OFDM, this leaks each subcarrier s into its mirror image −s. Ordinary precoders and combiners ignore that leakage.
  • Impairments on both ends of the link. Earlier work typically assumes an ideal transmitter. This design models IQ imbalance at both the transmitting UEs and the receiving BSs, and builds the image-subcarrier coupling into the precoder, the combiner, and the RIS design.
  • Low complexity. UE precoders are found by bisection and BS combiners in closed form. The multi-RIS configuration, which couples each subcarrier pair (s, −s) through one frequency-flat reflection matrix, is solved globally by a block coordinate descent with closed-form bisection updates.
  • Robust in practice. With imperfect channel estimates the design stays robust, and the gain from extra BS antennas is preserved.

System architecture

RIS-assisted cell-free uplink. K multi-antenna UEs reach C base stations over direct links and through Q passive RISs. The BSs forward their signals over fronthaul to a central processing unit that performs fully centralized combining. Every UE transmitter and every BS receiver suffers from IQ imbalance.

Approach

The design maximizes the uplink sum-rate of a fully centralized, multi-user cell-free MIMO-OFDM network. It jointly optimizes UE precoding, multi-RIS coefficients, and BS combining under two-sided IQ imbalance. The problem is nonconvex, so we developed a WMMSE-based block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm:

  • BS combiners: closed-form updates.
  • MSE weights: closed-form updates.
  • UE precoders: solved by bisection search under each UE’s power budget.
  • Multi-RIS coefficients: the subproblem couples subcarrier pairs (s, −s) through a single frequency-flat reflection matrix. It is reformulated and solved globally with a low-cost BCD that uses closed-form, bisection-based updates.
  • Guarantees and complexity: every block subproblem is convex, so the BCD converges to a stationary solution. The per-iteration complexity is derived.

Results

Beats classical, heuristic, and impairment-blind designs. Under level-3 IQ imbalance, the proposed design delivers 11–47× the sum-rate of conventional WMMSE that ignores IQ imbalance, up to 40% more than classical MMSE, and up to 3.4× more than an IQ-imbalance-aware design with random RIS settings. The gains are largest at low transmit power.
Scales with antennas and users. Under level-3 IQ imbalance, the sum-rate grows with transmit power and BS antennas (16 to 32). With 8 UEs and 32 antennas it exceeds 80 bps/Hz at 10 dBm.
Graceful degradation with impairment severity. The sum-rate decreases as IQ imbalance grows from level 1 (±10°, amplitude 0.9–1.1) to level 3 (±30°, amplitude 0.7–1.3). More BS antennas raise the rate at every level.
Robust to imperfect channel knowledge. The design is computed on estimated channels and evaluated on the true ones. Low and high estimation uncertainty reduce the sum-rate, but the design stays robust and keeps the gain from more BS antennas.
Converges in about 30 iterations with four UEs and level-3 IQ imbalance at −5 dBm.

Simulation testbed

Implemented in MATLAB, using:

  • Numerical computing: MATLAB’s built-in linear algebra (BLAS/LAPACK)
  • Figure regeneration: Python with NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib

Size:

  • About 31,600 lines of MATLAB across 213 files over the project’s development (about 20,300 lines of code). The final simulation testbed is about 12,000 lines.
  • About 1,100 lines of Python for the figures.

Simulation parameters

  • Network:
    • 4 BSs at (±30, ±30, 3) m with 16–32 receive antennas each.
    • 2 RISs at (±40, 175, 10) m with 81 elements each, as uniform planar arrays.
    • UEs placed randomly within 30 m of (0, 350, 1.5) m, each with 4 antennas and 4 streams.
  • Band: 28 GHz carrier, 12 subcarriers with 15 kHz spacing (180 kHz).
  • Channel: Rician (κ = 10) with 4 taps and 4 paths per tap.
  • Path loss: 3GPP model with exponent 2.2 for links through a RIS and 4.8 for direct UE–BS links.
  • IQ imbalance: amplitude and phase drawn uniformly from:
    • level 1: 0.9–1.1 and ±10°
    • level 2: 0.8–1.2 and ±20°
    • level 3: 0.7–1.3 and ±30°
