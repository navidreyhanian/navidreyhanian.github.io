Symbol-Level Mask-Compliant Hybrid Precoding for Multi-User MIMO-OFDM Systems

Hybrid precoding that guarantees spectral-mask and clipping compliance for every transmitted OFDM symbol and tolerates phase-shifter errors. mmWave MU-MIMO-OFDM, 28 GHz.

Paper: N. Reyhanian, P. Ramezani, and E. Björnson, “Symbol-Level Mask-Compliant Hybrid Precoding for Multi-User MIMO-OFDM Systems,” arXiv:2604.17853 (PDF).

Why it matters for modem and RF system design

  • Guaranteed compliance, not best-effort suppression. Many existing designs simply push out-of-band emissions down as far as they can. This design enforces the clipping limit and the emission mask for every transmitted symbol tuple, so the spectrum is guaranteed to meet whatever mask is required.
  • Per-symbol peak control. OFDM peaks drive clipping, distortion, and spectral regrowth. The per-antenna amplitude limit is enforced on every realized waveform, not on average.
  • A receiver split that matches real hardware. The transmitter’s digital precoders adapt to each symbol tuple. The users’ analog and digital combiners are symbol-agnostic and are updated from batch statistics.
  • Scalable and impairment-aware. A four-block ADMM uses closed-form or bisection steps that parallelize across antennas or subcarriers. Each phase shifter gets a closed-form update, with a robust version for phase errors. Convergence and complexity are analyzed.

System architecture

Symbol-level hybrid MU-MIMO-OFDM chain. For each realized symbol tuple, the AP's digital precoder Vks(ω) shapes the waveform so that the power, per-antenna clipping, and emission-mask limits all hold. IFFT + CP, DACs, and RF chains drive one phase shifter per antenna. Each user applies RF combining, ADCs, CP removal + FFT, and a symbol-agnostic digital combiner. The design minimizes the sum-MSE across users and subcarriers.

Approach

The design minimizes the downlink sum mean-squared error over all users and subcarriers. The problem is nonconvex in every variable, so we developed an MMSE-based block coordinate descent (BCD) algorithm that updates the blocks in Gauss-Seidel order:

  • Digital precoder (per symbol tuple): a large convex problem constrained by the power budget, per-antenna mask and clipping limits, and the linear relations between frequency- and time-domain, per-antenna, and per-RF-chain variables. A four-block ADMM solves it without forming the precoders explicitly. Every sub-step has a closed form or a bisection search, and several run in parallel across antennas or subcarriers.
  • RF precoder and analog combiners: coordinate descent with a closed-form update per phase shifter. Robust variants minimize the expected MSE under random phase errors.
  • Digital combiners: closed-form MMSE updates computed from batch statistics, so they don’t depend on individual symbols.
  • Guarantees: the regularized objective is monotonically nonincreasing and convergent, and its accumulation points are coordinatewise stationary. The per-block complexity is derived.

Results

Compliant with every mask. The transmitted PSD at antenna 1 stays under five different emission masks. They range from a relaxed −60 → −70 dBm/100 kHz ramp to a flat −90 dBm/100 kHz limit.
Where conventional precoders fail the mask. Hybrid ZF and MRT, including Taheri et al.'s frequency notching, leave out-of-band sidelobes 46–60 dB above the mask. The proposed design keeps the spectrum under it (NRF = 16, Ps = 30 dBm).
Lower emissions and far lower error. From 15 to 35 dBm, the sum-MSE is 65–83× lower than all four ZF/MRT baselines. The gain comes from jointly optimizing the precoders together with each user's combiners.
Beats published RF designs. The proposed design has the lowest sum-MSE at every power and RF-chain count. It is up to 15.8% below Riemannian conjugate gradient (RCG), up to 6.6% below Wang et al.'s coordinate descent, and 30–60% below random phases.
Built for imperfect hardware. With phase-shifter errors of 3°, 6°, and 9°, the robust update rules lower the sum-MSE by 5–14% compared with the non-robust design at every tested power (NRF = 16).
Emissions on demand. Moving from Mask 1 to Mask 5 drives out-of-band emissions from −46.93 to −69.25 dBm, while in-band power stays at 6.49–6.65 dBm. The cost is a sum-MSE rise from 0.0654 to 0.198 (Ps = 25 dBm, NRF = 16).
Where the mask bites. Per-user EVM is lowest on the central subcarriers and rises at the band edges, where tighter masks demand the strongest suppression.
Link-level check. Per-subcarrier symbol-error rate for 16- to 1024-QAM. The SER falls with transmit power and with lower modulation order.
Fast, monotone convergence. The sum-MSE falls by about two orders of magnitude from initialization within 20–30 BCD iterations (25 dBm per subcarrier, 64 subcarriers).
Scales with power and RF chains. The sum-MSE decreases with transmit power and with more RF chains (8, 16, 32).

Simulation testbed

Implemented in Python with an object-oriented design, using:

  • Numerical computing: NumPy and SciPy on optimized BLAS/LAPACK, plus scikit-sparse for sparse Cholesky factorizations
  • Channel modeling: NVIDIA Sionna on TensorFlow
  • Parallelization: multiprocessing (process pools across all CPU cores), concurrent.futures, and threading
  • Plotting: Matplotlib

Size: about 30,500 lines of Python across 46 modules, of which about 19,000 are code.

Simulation parameters

  • Arrays and users: 32-antenna BS; 4 users with 4 antennas, 2 RF chains, and 2 streams each, sharing all subcarriers. Half-wavelength ULAs.
  • Band: 28 GHz carrier, 20 MHz bandwidth, 64 subcarriers.
  • Symbols: 64-QAM unless stated, with batches of 30 symbol realizations.
  • Geometry and channel: users within a 4 m radius, 100 m from the BS. Frequency-selective Rician channel (\(\kappa\) = 10 dB) with 3GPP path loss and shadowing.
  • Noise: −174 dBm/Hz with an 8 dB noise figure.
  • Constraints: clipping limit \(\chi = 3\). The default mask is inactive below 10.01 MHz, ramps from −70 to −80 dBm/100 kHz between 10.01 and 12.5 MHz, and is flat at −80 dBm/100 kHz beyond that. It is enforced at 90 frequency samples per side.
